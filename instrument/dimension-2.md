# Dimension 2: Throughput Legitimacy

*Institutional/procedural dimension. Covers perceived inclusiveness of decision-making processes, perceived accountability of elected representatives, perceived responsiveness of political institutions to citizen input, perceived concentration of power in the executive branch, and perceived efficacy of institutional checks on government power.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Two forms** | Form A and Form B are parallel forms measuring the same constructs in different words. Form order is randomised per participant and fixed across two waves, pre- and post-treatment. |
| **Two levels** | Each construct can be tested for national-level or EU-level programmes. |
| **Response scale** | 5-point agree/disagree Likert scale items. |
| **Measurement model** | Provisionally reflective — the five items are expected to correlate. See [`scale-standardisation.md`](../methodology/scale-standardisation.md). |

> [!NOTE]
> This dimension measures the *perceived quality of a system*, so a low score may accurately describe the system rather than a deficit in the respondent, whose scepticism may be a mark of democratic competence. Inference is drawn from change within individuals against a comparison group in the same context, never from levels.

All items use the Agreement anchor family: *strongly disagree · disagree · neither agree nor disagree · agree · strongly agree*.

---

## 2.1 Perceived inclusiveness of decision-making processes

*Not reverse-scored.*

### 2.1.1 — National level

**Form A.** People who are usually not involved in politics still have ways to make themselves heard in politics in my country.

**Form B.** The concerns of minorities or marginalized groups are taken seriously in politics in my country.

### 2.1.2 — EU level

**Form A.** People who are usually not involved in politics still have ways to make themselves heard in EU-level political decision-making.

**Form B.** The concerns of minorities or marginalized groups are taken seriously in EU-level political decision-making.

---

## 2.2 Perceived accountability of elected representatives

*Not reverse-scored.*

### 2.2.1 — National level

**Form A.** Elected officials in my country face real consequences, such as losing office, when they act against the public interest.

**Form B.** There are effective ways for citizens in my country to complain about or challenge an elected official's conduct.

### 2.2.2 — EU level

**Form A.** Members of the European Parliament and other EU officials face real consequences, such as losing office, when they act against the public interest.

**Form B.** There are effective ways for citizens to complain about or challenge an EU official's conduct.

---

## 2.3 Perceived responsiveness of political institutions to citizen input

> [!WARNING]
> **Reverse-scored** — recode `6 − raw score`. Agreeing with these items signals *low* throughput legitimacy.

### 2.3.1 — National level

**Form A.** The government in my country rarely changes its policies, even when a large number of citizens want it to.

**Form B.** Lobbying and special interest groups shape government decisions in my country more than ordinary citizens' preferences do.

### 2.3.2 — EU level

**Form A.** EU institutions rarely change their policies, even when a large number of citizens want them to.

**Form B.** Lobbying and special interest groups shape EU-level decisions more than ordinary citizens' preferences do.

---

## 2.4 Perceived concentration of power in the executive branch

> [!WARNING]
> **Reverse-scored** — recode `6 − raw score`. Agreeing with these items signals *low* throughput legitimacy.

### 2.4.1 — National level

**Form A.** The most important government decisions in my country are made by the government alone.

**Form B.** The president or prime minister of my country can make major decisions without approval from parliament or a similar elected body.

### 2.4.2 — EU level

**Form A.** The most important EU-level decisions are made by the European Commission alone.

**Form B.** The European Commission can make major decisions without approval from the European Parliament or the Council of the EU.

---

## 2.5 Perceived efficacy of institutional checks on government power

*Not reverse-scored.*

### 2.5.1 — National level

**Form A.** If courts in my country would rule against the government, the government would follow their decision.

**Form B.** Independent bodies, such as auditors and the press, can investigate the government in my country without political interference.

### 2.5.2 — EU level

**Form A.** If the Court of Justice of the EU would rule against a decision made by the European Commission, the Commission would follow the ruling.

**Form B.** Independent bodies, such as auditors and the press, can investigate EU institutions without political interference.
