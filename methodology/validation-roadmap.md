## What the two pilots are for

**Pilot study A** establishes that the instrument is stable and that the two forms are equivalent. It runs with no intervention, so any change between waves is measurement noise rather than effect. It supplies the test-retest evidence that [`scale-standardisation.md`](scale-standardisation.md) §4 identifies as the primary reliability evidence for the formative dimensions, and it is where form-difficulty differences are caught and corrected.

**Pilot study B** is the first use of the instrument as designed: a treated cohort and a waitlist comparison cohort, pre-registered, across languages. It supplies the factor structure and measurement invariance evidence.

## What changes once validation completes

Until the validation paper is published, the instrument is unvalidated and results carry the provisional labelling required by [`administration-and-validation.md`](administration-and-validation.md) §11. Afterwards, three things may change — use and report the version current when you field:

- **Weighting** may become factor-derived rather than equal.
- **Items** may be replaced where they fail to discriminate or add no information, logged in [`CHANGELOG.md`](../CHANGELOG.md).
- **Form equivalence**, once established, makes counterbalancing recommended rather than mandatory. Until that appears in the changelog, §3 of the administration file applies in full.
